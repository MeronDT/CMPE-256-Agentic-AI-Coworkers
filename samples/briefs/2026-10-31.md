## 1. Learning to Formulate: Agentic Hardware-Software Co-Design of AI Infrastructure through Progressive Optimization and Verification

**Source:** arXiv | **Published:** 2026-10-29 | **Read:** 5 min
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39380v1

The article describes AI infrastructure design as layered, which makes systems manageable but also prevents an optimizer from considering hardware–software co-evolution. It proposes “Formulaic,” a flow that splits responsibilities: an agent formulates, a solver optimizes under those assumptions, and verification/measurement check correctness and results while the agent refines step-by-step from computation graph toward the chip. It reports faster layer runtimes and improved worst-case cycle counts from joint hardware–software design, plus speedups from reusing proofs during a multi-step refinement chain.

**Key insights**

- Use a structured flow that separates (1) formulation, (2) optimization, and (3) verification/measurement to turn agent proposals into checkable designs. *("“We present Formulaic, a flow that divides the work. The agent writes the formulation, a mathematical solver optimizes within it, and verification and measurement check its assumptions and results.”")*
- Model refinement as “templates” (families of programs with correctness conditions), and search chip choices via wall relaxation that prices binding hardware limits. *("“Each step is a template: a family of programs with the conditions under which they are correct. Wall relaxation searches the chip by pricing the hardware limits that bind the software.”")*
- When refining computation→chip in multiple steps (e.g., GEMM refinement chain), reusing proofs can make checking substantially faster. *("“On a five-step GEMM refinement chain, reusing proofs makes checking $2.51\times$ faster.”")*

**Takeaway:** When using agentic optimization for infrastructure, constrain the search with a formal formulation and then make each refinement step verifiable (using verification/measurement and reusable proofs) instead of relying on the agent to prove correctness implicitly.

**Adds to your knowledge:** Adds a concrete co-design pattern: agent-generated formulations constrain a solver, while verification/measurement closes the loop to validate assumptions and results during iterative refinement toward the chip.

## 2. A Systematic Review of Modern Machine Learning in Query Optimization

**Source:** ACM Computing Surveys | **Published:** 2026-10-31 | **Read:** 5 min
**URL:** https://doi.org/10.1145/3847509

The article is a PRISMA-guided systematic review of recent (2018–2025) studies applying machine learning to query optimization. It focuses on where learning-based methods intervene in the classical optimization pipeline and what tradeoffs remain around generalization, benchmark standardization, and deployment readiness. Overall, it reports a shift from isolated predictors toward integrated optimizers, but notes persistent gaps for real-world readiness.

**Key insights**

- The review organizes learning-based query optimization methods into five explicit intervention points across the classical pipeline. *("We synthesize the literature across five intervention points: component-level estimation, learned enumeration, hint steering, end-to-end optimization, and emerging trends.")*
- A key takeaway is a paradigm shift from isolated predictors toward integrated optimizers. *("Results show a paradigm shift from isolated predictors to integrated optimizers, while gaps in generalization and standardized evaluation persist.")*
- Even with progress, limitations remain in three areas: generalization, benchmark standardization, and deployment readiness. *("addressing two research questions: (RQ1) how learning-based approaches intervene across the classical optimization pipeline and what tradeoffs characterize each stage, and (RQ2) what limitations persist in generalization, benchmark standardization, and deployment readiness.")*

**Takeaway:** When evaluating ML-based query optimization work, map the method to one of the five intervention points and then explicitly check generalization, benchmark standardization, and deployment readiness.

**Adds to your knowledge:** Adds a structured framework for reasoning about where ML plugs into query optimization (estimation, enumeration, hint steering, end-to-end optimization) and what evaluation gaps to look for.

## 3. Vessel-specific voyage optimization with machine learning: Manta on AWS

**Source:** AWS Architecture | **Published:** 2026-10-27 | **Read:** 5 min
**URL:** https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/architecture/vessel-specific-voyage-optimization-with-machine-learning-manta-on-aws/

Manta addresses inaccurate voyage predictions that arise when models are trained on vessel-type averages rather than each vessel’s own history. On AWS, it builds a cloud-native data infrastructure that ingests vessel and environmental data, orchestrates ETL and ML workloads, and continuously retrains per-vessel models. The system uses Apache Airflow (on a persistent EC2 instance) to run workloads as Amazon ECS tasks and integrates AWS services for storage, security, and observability. The result is voyage guidance that becomes more tailored as new voyage data arrives.

**Key insights**

- Manta dedicates capacity per instance type (separate capacity provider/ASG/launch template) to control scaling and workload urgency. *("Manta defines one capacity provider, one Auto Scaling group, and one launch template per instance type instead of sharing a single pool.")*
- It uses a persistent EC2 Airflow instance because orchestration needs a long-running server process. *("Airflow runs on a persistent EC2 instance because the orchestration layer needs a long-running server process.")*
- Manta improves cost-performance by running workloads on AWS Graviton-based instances, with measurable price performance gains. *("Manta observed approximately 25–30 percent better price performance after moving to Graviton-based instances.")*

**Takeaway:** When orchestrating ML/ETL at scale, consider dedicating infrastructure capacity per workload/instance type (with explicit scaling choices) and keep your orchestration layer on a long-running host if it must manage ongoing workflows.

**Adds to your knowledge:** Adds a concrete AWS reference architecture pattern for ML pipeline orchestration: persistent Airflow on EC2 submitting compute to ECS tasks, combined with per-instance-type capacity management and cost optimization via Graviton.
